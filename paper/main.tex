\begin{abstract}
Structure-from-motion (SfM) pipelines still fail on scenes with wide
baselines, low overlap, repeated texture, and doppelganger views, because
a fixed matcher cannot adapt its strategy to the scene. We study whether
a small multimodal language model can \emph{orchestrate} a toolbox of
geometric specialists — dense and sparse matchers (LoFTR, MASt3R), a
feed-forward pose prior (VGGT), doppelganger filtering, and COLMAP —
rather than replace them. The policy is trained on-policy with GRPO under
a fully machine-verifiable reward derived from ground-truth camera poses,
and consolidated with a direction-constrained SFT variant (OPSFT) that
restricts post-RL supervised updates to the sign and support of the RL
update. Our preliminary findings are honest and mixed: (i) specialist
heterogeneity is real and large — on 1,170 frozen ScanNet pairs the VGGT
pose head reaches AUC@10$^\circ$ 0.631 and MASt3R 0.506 versus 0.240 for
LoFTR — establishing that \emph{which} tool is called matters;
(ii) a 2B-parameter policy can be RL-trained end-to-end in the loop
(mean scene reward 0.85$\to$1.64 over 50 epochs), but the current warm
start collapses matcher choice to LoFTR and never explores the stronger
specialists; and (iii) direction-constrained SFT is implementable for
LoRA adapters, though exact RL update support is near-dense under AdamW
(99.998\%), motivating sparse top-$k$ support approximations. We release
the environment, reward, frozen evaluation manifest, and direction
artifacts, and outline a staged plan on 8$\times$H200 to determine whether
learned budget-aware routing over specialists beats equally-budgeted fixed
baselines.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Structure-from-motion remains a prerequisite for 3D reconstruction,
localization, and neural rendering, yet classical pipelines are brittle:
correspondence estimators fail on low-overlap and wide-baseline pairs,
repeated texture produces doppelganger views that corrupt the view graph,
and incremental reconstruction can split a scene into disconnected
components. Each of these failure modes has a specialized remedy —
better matchers~\cite{loftr,mast3r}, feed-forward camera/geometry
priors~\cite{vggt}, doppelganger classifiers~\cite{doppelgangerspp}, and
bundle adjustment~\cite{colmap} — but selecting \emph{which} remedy to
apply, on \emph{which} image pairs, under a \emph{limited compute budget}
is itself a sequential decision problem that no fixed pipeline solves.

Recent work in reinforcement learning for vision-language models (RLVR)
shows that small multimodal models can learn verifiable tool-use
behavior~\cite{visualrft,dapo}. This suggests a reframing of SfM: keep the
geometric specialists frozen and exact, and learn a \emph{policy} that
orchestrates them — constructing the view graph, choosing matchers per
edge, pruning doppelgangers, bridging components, invoking reconstruction,
and deciding when to stop. This idea was articulated publicly by
Berton~\cite{bertonidea}; to our knowledge no trained system has yet been
reported, and the closest concurrent work applies RL to direct matching
rather than tool orchestration~\cite{reasonmatch}.

We build that system and report what is true so far — including the parts
that do not yet work:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em,itemsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Environment.} A scene-level SfM environment where a
  Qwen3.5-2B policy issues structured tool calls against a stateful tool
  server hosting LoFTR, MASt3R, VGGT, doppelganger checks, and COLMAP
  (\S\ref{sec:env}). Rewards are computed from ground-truth camera poses —
  no human or learned preference labels.
  \item \textbf{Measured specialist heterogeneity.} On 1,170 pairs from a
  frozen 30-scene ScanNet manifest, VGGT's extrinsics head attains
  AUC@10$^\circ$ of 0.631 (median error $6.79^\circ$), MASt3R 0.506
  ($6.65^\circ$), and LoFTR 0.240 ($68.85^\circ$) — a $2.6\times$ spread
  that makes per-edge tool selection a real decision
  (\S\ref{sec:specialist-bench}).
  \item \textbf{A trained scene policy, honestly assessed.} GRPO trains
  the agent in the loop (mean reward $0.847 \!\to\! 1.643$; best held-out
  scene score 0.608) but the SFT warm start collapses matcher selection to
  LoFTR — a specialization of \emph{interaction collapse}
  \S\ref{sec:hybrid}. We show where the entropy went and what fixes it.
  \item \textbf{OPSFT for adapters.} We adapt direction-constrained SFT
  \cite{opsft} from full-parameter to LoRA training, implement exact and
  top-$k$ support masks, and report the surprising near-density of AdamW
  LoRA update support ($99.998\%$), which changes what the constraint can
  mean (\S\ref{sec:opsft}).
  \item \textbf{A frozen, auditable protocol.} 30 held-out ScanNet scenes
  with a content-hashed manifest, equal-budget baselines through an
  identical reconstruction path, and explicit gates that must pass before
  any routing claim (\S\ref{sec:protocol}).
\end{itemize}

Our central claim is deliberately narrow at this stage: \emph{learned,
budget-aware orchestration over classical and feed-forward 3D specialists
is a feasible training problem}, and the infrastructure, verifiable reward,
and evaluation gates exist to answer it. Whether the trained router beats
the best equally-budgeted fixed matcher is exactly what the frozen protocol
is designed to decide; we do not claim it does.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{RL for vision-language models.}
GRPO~\cite{grpo} and its descendants (DAPO's clip-higher and dynamic
sampling~\cite{dapo}; Visual-RFT~\cite{visualrft}) established that small
VLMs improve under verifiable rewards. S-GRPO~\cite{sgrpo} unifies SFT and
RL via conditional ground-truth trajectory injection when all rollouts in
a group fail; we adopt both its injection mechanism and its motivation
that naive SFT-then-RL can lock in a single expert mode (cf.\ DCRL's
finding that SFT can hurt transfer~\cite{reasonmatch}). PyVision-RL
documents \emph{interaction collapse} — policies that reduce tool usage
under RL — and proposes accumulative tool reward~\cite{pyvisionrl}; our
observed matcher-choice collapse is a categorical analogue on the tool
\emph{identity} axis rather than the tool \emph{count} axis.

\paragraph{Agentic tool use.}
Multi-turn visual tool agents (VISTA-Gym~\cite{vista}, MGPO~\cite{mgpo},
NTEP~\cite{ntep}) show grounding and evidence-seeking can emerge from
outcome rewards alone, and LiteSearch-VL~\cite{litesearch} shows a small
model must first learn the ``agent contract'' — valid tool-call syntax —
via a minimal SFT stage, which our pipeline implements.

\paragraph{SfM, matching, and feed-forward 3D.}
LoFTR~\cite{loftr} remains a strong frozen matcher; MASt3R~\cite{mast3r}
and VGGT~\cite{vggt} regress dense point maps and camera parameters
feed-forward. Doppelganger detection~\cite{doppelgangerspp,xdg} and global
SfM with 3D foundation models (GLOMAP~\cite{glomap}, Glob3R~\cite{glob3r})
supply complementary specialists and baselines. ReasonMatch/DCRL
\cite{reasonmatch} is the closest RL work but trains the MLLM to
\emph{match directly} with 220k pairs — no tools, cropping, or SfM. Our
contribution is complementary: the MLLM never produces correspondences
itself; it decides \emph{which specialist runs where and when}.

\paragraph{Constrained post-training.}
OPSFT~\cite{opsft} shows that the \emph{direction} of on-policy RL updates
carries the generalization signal: constraining SFT to that support and
sign reproduces RL-level behavior. We adapt it to LoRA adapters
(\S\ref{sec:opsft}), where — unlike the paper's full-parameter regime —
AdamW makes the exact support nearly dense.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Problem formulation}
\label{sec:env}

An SfM episode is a tool-augmented Markov decision process. The state $s_t$
is the current view graph: registered images, per-edge correspondence
caches, component structure, and reconstruction summaries. The policy
$\pi_\theta$ (Qwen3.5-2B, LoRA $r{=}32$, $\alpha{=}64$) observes a
bounded set of images plus the textual graph state and emits one
structured action per turn. Actions call tools on a stateful FastAPI
server (\tool{crop}, \tool{match}, \tool{retrieve},
\tool{build\_initial\_graph}, \tool{inspect\_graph},
\tool{verify\_doppelganger}, \tool{prune\_edges},
\tool{bridge\_components}, \tool{sfm\_run}, \tool{inspect}, \tool{done});
each \tool{sfm\_run} executes COLMAP in a child process so a backend
abort cannot kill the server. The matcher choice set is
$\{\text{LoFTR}, \text{MASt3R}, \text{VGGT}\}$, exposed to the policy as a
\emph{parameter of the match/graph actions}, so routing is a learned
decision rather than a pipeline constant. Budgets are capped
(20 tool calls, 30 turns per scene episode).

Crucially, ground-truth poses are used \emph{only} by the reward function
— never exposed to the policy — so the agent must infer progress from
tool outputs (match counts, registration counts, confidence).

\subsection{Verifiable reward}
\label{sec:reward}

The scene reward combines (i) registered-fraction credit, (ii) pose
accuracy credit ($1 - \bar{e}/20^\circ$ clipped, with $\bar{e}$ the mean
relative-pose angular error), (iii) a split penalty for zero-registration
episodes, (iv) a doppelganger term scored against impostor edges derived
from the covisibility matrix (no self-certification), (v) a bounded
graph-sparsity cost applied only at high coverage, and (vi) PyVision-RL's
accumulative tool reward — a bonus proportional to tool-call count paid
\emph{only} on successful outcomes, replacing the per-call penalty that
incentivizes interaction collapse~\cite{pyvisionrl}. A first-call validity
gate prevents degenerate opening calls from winning a GRPO group; we note
in \S\ref{sec:limitations} that this bonus still distorts the reward scale.

\subsection{On-policy training}
\label{sec:grpo}

We train with GRPO over groups of $G{=}4$ scene rollouts
(Calder config: $G{=}6$) with group-normalized advantages, PPO-style
clipping with asymmetric clip-high $0.28$, DAPO dynamic sampling, a frozen
KL reference adapter (coefficient $0.03$, K3 estimator), and S-GRPO
conditional injection of the oracle trajectory when every rollout in a
group fails. Rollouts run on vLLM with the trainable LoRA hot-reloaded
each step; training and the frozen KL leash occupy separate GPUs.
Training is LoRA-only on bf16 with AdamW ($\mathrm{lr}{=}5\!\times\!10^{-6}$).

\subsection{Direction-constrained SFT (OPSFT for adapters)}
\label{sec:opsft}

Let $\theta_{\mathrm{base}}$ be the SFT adapter GRPO started from and
$\theta_{\mathrm{on}}$ the RL endpoint. Following OPSFT~\cite{opsft} we
extract a support mask $M$ and elementwise sign
$D = \operatorname{sign}(\theta_{\mathrm{on}}-\theta_{\mathrm{base}})$
(computed in float32 to avoid bf16 cancellation). Constrained SFT then
enforces two projections per step: \textbf{(1)} before the optimizer step,
$g \leftarrow g \odot M$ and $g_i \leftarrow 0$ wherever the descent
movement opposes $D$ ($g_i D_i > 0$); \textbf{(2)} after the step,
parameters outside $M$ are restored to $\theta_0$ and parameters that
crossed against $D$ are snapped back. Because the paper's exact-support
definition proved near-dense under AdamW LoRA training
($99.998\%$ of elements differ; \S\ref{sec:support-density}) we introduce
a \emph{top-$k$} mask selecting the global top-$f$ fraction of
$|\Delta\theta|$ (we use $f{=}0.1$), recovering a genuinely sparse
constraint. Two arms exist: \emph{from-base} (constrained SFT reproducing
the RL direction from scratch) and \emph{continue} (constrained SFT
extending a GRPO checkpoint).

\subsection{Evaluation protocol}
\label{sec:protocol}

The scene is the statistical unit. Thirty ScanNet scenes
(\texttt{scene0715\_00} onward, 20 frames each, content-hashed manifest)
are frozen and excluded from all training; MegaDepth 0015/0022 are
training scenes and never appear in held-out claims. Metrics: registered
fraction (unregistered images count as failures), relative-pose
AUC@5/10/20 over all pairs, component count, and failure taxonomy.
Baselines run through the \emph{same} cached-match reconstruction path at
equal tool budget: consecutive frames, random pairs, top-$k$ retrieval
($k{=}5,10$), direct matching, a GT-overlap oracle, the SFT-only
checkpoint, and each fixed specialist. Superiority requires a scene-level
paired bootstrap interval excluding zero (\S\ref{sec:limitations} lists
why the earlier 10-scene protocol could not support claims).

\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:exp}

\subsection{Setup}

All Aire runs use 3$\times$L40S (48\,GB): GPU\,0 vLLM rollout, GPU\,1
LoRA training, GPU\,2 matcher + COLMAP server. The Calder staging
(\S\ref{sec:calder-plan}) scales to one 8$\times$H200 node with vLLM
tensor-parallel inference at 16k context. Unless stated otherwise, numbers
are from the artifacts named in each table.

\subsection{Do specialists differ enough to make routing worth learning?}
\label{sec:specialist-bench}

Before any routing claim we measured the raw material: identical pair
lists (19 consecutive + 20 seeded-random per scene, 1,170 pairs) over the
frozen 30-scene ScanNet manifest, GT intrinsics, IMC-style worst-of-two
angular error. Failures and fallbacks count as failures.

Table~\ref{tab:specialists} is the empirical premise of this project:
specialists disagree by $2$--$3\times$ in AUC and by an order of magnitude
in latency (LoFTR 0.06\,s vs.\ VGGT/MASt3R $\sim$1.26\,s median), so a
router that spends expensive calls only where cheap ones fail could beat
every fixed choice per budget. Two caveats bound the claim: this ordering
is measured on ScanNet and may not transfer to outdoor MegaDepth
(different overlap/texture statistics), and VGGT lists ScanNet among its
training corpora — overlap with our frozen scenes is unlikely but
unconfirmed~\cite{vggt}.

\subsection{Zero-shot and pair-level RL (Phases 0--1)}

\textbf{Phase 0 (negative).} Stock Qwen3.5-2B with a crop tool does not
beat direct matching on hard MegaDepth pairs (mean inliers 8.9 vs.\ 12.4;
pose AUC unchanged at 0.017). On real scenes 0015/0022 the zero-shot
policy degenerated to full-frame crops, so agent and direct matching are
identical there. Conclusion: the tool-use prior must be \emph{trained}.

\textbf{Phase 1 (negative).} Pair-level GRPO on the hard-pair split,
warm-started from oracle-crop SFT, was stopped at $\sim$200 steps at the
2-day wall-clock limit (final logged training reward 1.493, including a
+0.5 first-call bonus — not comparable to baselines). Its best checkpoint
underperforms the fixed matcher on the hard validation split ($n{=}50$):
62\% success vs.\ 88\% for plain LoFTR and 90\% for an oracle crop;
geometric reward 0.595 vs.\ 0.965. The trained agent also falls below
LoFTR on the full 153-pair validation set (58\% success). We report these
as negative results: at the pair level, learned orchestration did not beat
the matcher it orchestrates.

\subsection{Scene-level GRPO and the matcher-choice collapse}
\label{sec:hybrid}

\textbf{Pipeline (Phase 2).} The scene agent runs the tool grammar of
\S\ref{sec:env} over 20-image scenes with the composite reward of
\S\ref{sec:reward}. An initial 50-epoch LoFTR-only run (job 8073061)
completed and produced a functioning scene policy; its accompanying
10-scene evaluation reached only 30\% success, and we subsequently showed
that protocol invalid for benchmark purposes (two training scenes inside
the eval; a direct baseline whose 0\% success was a missing-key artefact;
an ``oracle'' matching only five pairs). That audit, not the score,
motivated the frozen protocol of \S\ref{sec:protocol}.

\textbf{Hybrid run (8237240).} We then exposed all three matchers to the
policy and retrained 50 epochs on MegaDepth scenes 0015+0022
($3\times$L40S, $\sim$31\,h). Training reward rose from
$0.847$ (epoch 0) to $1.643$ (epoch 49) with a monotone-ish trend, and the
in-training evaluation peaked at epoch 25 (eval score 0.608; 100\% of the
2-scene eval set successful; mean registered fraction 0.70; mean pose
error $2.62^\circ$; Table~\ref{tab:hybrid}). Weight-space distance from
the SFT init grew to $0.259$ (L2 over LoRA) by epoch 50 — the adapter
genuinely trained.

\textbf{The collapse.} Despite MASt3R and VGGT being exposed and strictly
better on the indoor benchmark (Table~\ref{tab:specialists}), the policy
issued \emph{zero} calls to either across 50 epochs: matcher-token
probability had collapsed to LoFTR during the LoFTR-only SFT warm start,
and GRPO — an on-policy method — never sampled the alternatives, so no
learning signal existed for them. This is interaction collapse on the
tool-identity axis and is now directly measurable: per-step matcher
fractions $\{\text{loftr}, \text{mast3r}, \text{vggt}\}$ and fallback
counts are logged in the trainer. Fixes staged for Calder
(\S\ref{sec:calder-plan}): forced specialist exploration, mixed-specialist
SFT trajectories, and cost-shaped rewards that make LoFTR's failure mode
(68.85$^\circ$ median error on indoor pairs) expensive.

\subsection{Planned Calder 8$\times$H200 matrix}
\label{sec:calder-plan}

The staged Calder job dedicates GPUs 0--1 to vLLM (TP\,=\,2, 16k context —
the 8k cap caused 129 context-overflow episodes on Aire), GPU\,2 to the
tool server, GPUs 3--4 to GRPO + frozen KL leash, GPUs 5--6 to parallel
OPSFT arms (top-10\% and exact support), and GPU\,7 to an evaluation
server plus automatic frozen-manifest benchmarking at job end. The
curriculum mixes MegaDepth with 32 scene-disjoint ScanNet training scenes
and 8 validation scenes — verified zero-overlap with the 30 frozen scenes.
Planned rows (each one job): hybrid GRPO; single-specialist controls
(LoFTR-only / MASt3R-only / VGGT-only at identical budget — the fairness
gate for any routing claim); group-size, context, KL, and curriculum
ablations; frozen-manifest benchmarking of every arm; and an OPSFT vs.\
GRPO head-to-head. All outcomes are \pending{Calder runs; no numbers
exist yet}.

\section{Limitations and threats to validity}
\label{sec:limitations}

We deliberately surface these rather than bury them:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{No routing evidence yet.} The hybrid run never called
  MASt3R/VGGT, so it cannot support a routing claim; Table~
  \ref{tab:specialists} shows headroom, not realized benefit.
  \item \textbf{Prior held-out eval was invalid.} The 10-scene Phase-2
  evaluation contained training scenes, an artefactual 0\%-success direct
  arm, and a 5-pair ``oracle''. All headline claims await the frozen
  30-scene protocol.
  \item \textbf{Reward scale is inflated.} The +0.5 first-call validity
  bonus contributes roughly half of several episode rewards; reward curves
  are not quality curves.
  \item \textbf{Domain gap.} Specialist ordering is ScanNet-measured;
  MegaDepth ordering is unmeasured and could differ.
  \item \textbf{VGGT train/test overlap} with ScanNet-1500 is unconfirmed
  (its paper does not list excluded scene IDs)~\cite{vggt}.
  \item \textbf{OPSFT is an adaptation.} LoRA-scoped, bf16, AdamW
  $2\!\times\!10^{-5}$ vs.\ the paper's full-parameter FP32 at
  $10^{-7}$; its claims do not transfer directly.
  \item \textbf{Two MegaDepth scenes} carried all scene-level training so
  far; the Calder curriculum broadens this but data scale remains below
  concurrent work (220k pairs~\cite{reasonmatch}).
  \item \textbf{Phase-1 checkpoint integrity.} An earlier checkpoint
  persistence bug wrote adapters byte-identical to their init; affected
  endpoints were excluded from analysis.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion}

We built and trained a multimodal agent that orchestrates — rather than
replaces — the geometric specialists of SfM, with rewards verified from
camera geometry. The evidence so far supports three honest conclusions:
the specialists differ enough that routing is worth learning; a 2B policy
trains in the loop under GRPO but inherited a collapsed tool-choice
distribution that on-policy RL could not escape; and OPSFT-style
direction-constrained SFT is implementable on adapters but must respect
the near-dense support that AdamW produces. The frozen evaluation protocol
and 8$\times$H200 matrix are designed to turn the remaining question —
\emph{does learned routing beat the best equally-budgeted fixed
specialist?} — into a decidable experiment.
